# Leakvue: notes for Claude

## What this is

A companion app for **LeakVue**, American Leak Detection's laser water-level tester for pools, spas and fountains. It's built for **one ALD franchise** (the repo owner) and is used by their techs on **iPhone and iPad**. The owner is not a developer: explain Xcode/Apple steps one click at a time, and ask before anything that costs money or touches their Apple account.

The official LeakVue app has hard-to-read graphs and nothing a homeowner can understand. This app runs the same test and explains it:

- A LeakVue test is a **15-minute scan** (the tech can change the length) with the **pump ON**, then the same with the **pump OFF**, comparing the loss rates.
- After the leak is located, the tech **isolates** a line (plugs returns, main drain, etc.) and **rescans** to see if that was the whole leak or if there's a second one.
- The app turns the readings into gallons (from the pool's surface area), splits loss into normal evaporation vs. leak, explains pump ON vs OFF in plain English, and builds a homeowner report of phone-sized cards meant to be screenshotted and texted.

See README.md for the user-facing description and file map.

## How it's built (keep it this way)

- Plain HTML/CSS/JS, **no build step, no bundler, no framework**. Classic `<script>` tags in `index.html`, all code under `window.LV`. It must keep working when served as static files (GitHub Pages) and opened from disk.
- Storage: IndexedDB via `js/db.js` (stores `tests`, `settings`). Backups are JSON export/import.
- All math lives in `js/calc.js` (pure, Node-testable). Run `node tests/calc.test.js` after touching it (`npm test` runs it and the decode tests).
- Homeowner wording lives in `js/catalog.js` and `js/story.js`, so the tech screen and report always say the same thing.
- Charts are hand-written HTML/SVG in `js/charts.js`. Colours were validated for colour-blind safety: blue `#257de0` = leak, gray `#8893a0` = normal evaporation. Keep those meanings.
- The report (`js/views/report.js`) is always light, and each card should fit one phone screen (about 650–700 px tall at 390 px wide).
- **The homeowner report must stay simple (owner, 27 Nov 2026: "homeowners don't understand what's going on").** By default it is three plain cards: (1) is it leaking and how much (verdict, then **inches a day** as the big number, because inches per day is the industry standard and custom pool shapes make gallons unreliable (owner, 27 Nov 2026); then evaporation for comparison, gallons a day and bathtubs only if a size was entered, inches a week, monthly water cost if set), (2) where (the part in everyday words from `C.plain.parts`, or the pump on/off conclusion from `C.plain.where`, plus one "how we know" line), (3) what happens next (tech's message, tech, phone, fine print). No charts, legends, tables, evaporation splits or jargon on those cards. The old bars, rescans, graphs and numbers table are "technical pages" behind a per-test switch (`t.reportDetail`), off by default. Plain wording lives in `C.plain` (catalog.js) and `LV.story.plain()` (story.js). The tech screen still shows the full technical picture.
- **One card per test, between the answer card and the where card (owner, 27 Nov 2026: "1 scan per screenshot", "I like the line graph not the bar").** Each card: a big title the way techs say it ("Pool on", "Pool off", "Spa jets on", "Return lines closed off"; `C.plain.scanTitles`), a simple **line graph** of the water level during that test (dashed "water level at the start" line, the level line, an end dot, the scan length; no axis numbers), and the amount below: `-1.10″ inches a day` in **red with LEAKING** or **green with NORMAL** (owner's colours for these cards; the word is there so colour isn't the only signal). All the graphs share one scale (longest scan, biggest drop), so a steeper line is a faster drop. Cards are ~358×288 (wider than tall) so a screenshot stays readable when ServiceTitan shrinks it. Scans where water was added are left out. Built from `LV.story.scanBlocks()`; typed-in scans draw a straight line.
- **Never tell a homeowner "no leak found"** (owner, 27 Nov 2026: it makes them think we're wrong, because they called us since their water IS going down). Say **"Your pool is not leaking"** (owner's preferred wording; NOT LEAKING on the ServiceTitan picture), then agree with what they've seen: how many inches a week it drops, and that it's evaporation, plus what else makes it drop (splashing, swimmers, backwashing, waterfalls). Wording in `C.plain.normal`.
- When you add or rename a JS file, update the `<script>` list in `index.html` and the `ASSETS` list in `sw.js`, and bump `VERSION` in `sw.js`.

## How a LeakVue test is set up (from the owner, 26 Nov 2026)

It's an electronic bucket test built on a stilling well:
1. A weighted 4-way 1/4" pipe is lowered slowly to the pool floor on a 1/4" clear tube, bleeding all the air out.
2. The LeakVue canister is set at the pool edge; a red tape band around it gets it roughly to water height. The canister holds a float ("bopper") with a mirror; a laser reads the mirror's height.
3. The tube is connected to the canister, so the canister's water level follows the pool's, with waves damped out by the thin tube (hence the very steady readings).
4. In the ALD app, a fine screw raises/lowers the canister until the float sits in the middle of the **testing zone**. Outside the zone the device can't read (our `ff 7f ff ff` frames = float out of zone, not a fault).
5. Enter pool L×W and duration (default 15 min), press Go. The app shows a live graph and a projected 24-hour loss that firms up as the test runs (= least-squares slope × 24 h, the same idea as `LV.calc.scanRate`).

Air left in the tube, a float near the edge of the zone, or a bumped canister are the likely real-world causes of bad or frozen readings.

## Scans where the level goes UP (owner, 27 Nov 2026)

Scans can come out + (level rose) as well as −: an auto-fill or hose left on, rain, or water moving between bodies (spa spillover). A rise means water was being added, so it says nothing about a leak. `scanResult` marks it `rising`; `analyze` leaves rising scans out of baselines, rescans, the pump pattern and the worst case (`a.valid` / `a.risen`), and if every scan rose the status is `'rising'`. The tech screen flags it on the scan and in Results; the homeowner report says **"We need to test again"** (`C.plain.retest`), never "not leaking"; the ServiceTitan picture says RETEST NEEDED. Manual entry has a Down/Up switch (`scan.direction`) because the iPhone decimal keypad has no minus key; a typed minus still works.

**Number format (owner, 27 Nov 2026):** daily level change is written the industry way, negative with an inch mark: `-1.10″ inches a day` (`S.signedIn`). A body within evaporation reads "Not leaking · -0.27″ inches a day, within normal evaporation rate". Used on the report, the ServiceTitan picture and the test list.

## Scan settings and several bodies of water (owner, 27 Nov 2026)

- **What was running** is a per-scan setting, `scan.condition`, from `C.conditions`: entire system (pump off), pump on, water feature pump on, spa pump/jets on, cleaner/booster pump on, floor zone system on, basin pump on, basin pump off. Each has a `pump` side ('on' / 'off' / null). `scan.pump` is kept equal to that side (the test screen syncs it), and the pump ON vs OFF diagnosis uses only the on/off sides; null settings count for the worst case and show in the technical pages. Older scans with only `pump` are read as pump on / entire system. Assumption to confirm with the owner: "Entire system" means everything off.
- **Bodies of water:** a pool and spa may be tested as one system (one body, e.g. "Pool + spa" with the attached-spa area) or separately. The first body is still `test.pool` (id `'main'`), so old tests are unchanged; separately tested ones go in `test.otherBodies` (`{ id, body, shape, sizes… }`), and each scan has `scan.bodyId` (unknown → 'main'). `K.analyze(test, settings, body)` analyzes one body on its own scans and size, keeping the tech-screen scan numbers; `K.analyzeAll` does all; `K.headlineBody` picks the one to lead with (leak first, then retest, then not leaking). `S.bodyName` gives "Pool", "Spa", "Spa 2". With one body the screens and report are exactly as before; with several, the test screen has a "Which body of water?" field per scan and Results per body, the list shows "Pool + Spa" and "Pool: Leak · …", and the report's first card lists each body with its own verdict, one "where" card per leaking body, technical pages per body, and the ServiceTitan picture leads with the headline body ("Pool: up to 1.10 inches a day"). Size is optional for every body.

## The readings contract (where Bluetooth plugs in)

A scan is an object in `test.scans[]` (see `LV.model.newScan` in `js/views/tests.js`):

```js
{ id, bodyId, condition, pump: 'on'|'off'|null, purpose: 'baseline'|'isolation', isolated, compareTo,
  direction: 'down'|'up',       // manual entry only
  durationMin, dropIn,          // manual entry (inches), used when there are no readings
  readings: [[secondsSinceStart, levelInches], ...] | null,
  timerStartedAt }
```

If `readings` has 3 or more points, `LV.calc.scanRate` ignores `dropIn` and fits a least-squares trend line. The tech screen then shows "From LeakVue readings" and a level graph, and the report gets the graph card. **Level is in inches, and higher = more water.** Convert whatever the device sends into that before storing. Everything downstream already works; the sample test in `LV.model.sampleTest` uses simulated readings in exactly this shape.

### Phase 2: Device Lab (built; needs real captures)
`#/lab` (`js/views/lab.js`), linked as **Lab** in the app bar only inside the native app. It:
- Scans and lists nearby devices (name, id, signal strength, advertised services, maker data), strongest first. Names containing "leak" are highlighted and their advertisements logged on every change (**Watch** does the same for any device, in case the level is broadcast rather than read).
- Connects, lists every service and characteristic with its properties, and subscribes to everything that notifies or indicates. It does **not** read automatically: each readable characteristic has a **Read** button, because one unanswered read ends the connection (see the capture notes below). After the first unanswered read it disables reading for that connection (`STALLED`). Values that did answer can be re-read every 2 seconds.
- Leaves firmware-update services (`fef5`, Nordic DFU) completely alone: no subscribe, no read.
- **Writes exactly one thing, and only on a tap:** for the Dialog serial port service it offers **Tell the device we're ready**, which sends `01` (flow control ON, the published DSPS handshake) to `…cb9` without response and logs `WRITE`. Nothing else is ever written. Don't add other writes without the owner's say-so.
- If a read or subscribe fails with an authentication/encryption error it shows "Some values are locked", logs `LOCKED`, and goes no further.
- Logs each value with a timestamp as hex, ASCII and trial decodings (`js/decode.js`: u/i 16 and 32 bit and float32, both byte orders, at offsets aligned to the type size; decode other offsets from the hex). Characteristics get short aliases (C1, C2, …) defined by the `CHAR` lines.
- **Share log** writes a `.txt` to the cache folder and opens the share sheet (AirDrop to the Mac's Downloads). The header summarises count, distinct values and push rate per characteristic.
- Keeps the screen awake while open. The connection and log live in module state, so they survive switching screens but not quitting the app.

#### What the first real capture showed (26 Nov 2026, iPhone on iOS 18.7, built from Xcode without changes)

- The LeakVue 2 advertises as `LeakVue_0245` (short name `Leak`), connectable, with services `0783b03e-8535-b5a0-7140-a304d2495cb7` and `fef5`.
- Services after connecting: Device Information (`180a`); `fef5` with nine characteristics, which matches Dialog/Renesas **SUOTA** (firmware update over the air; never touch it); an unidentified service `866d3b04-e674-40dc-9c05-b7f91bec6e83` (two read/write characteristics and one read/notify/write); and `0783b03e-…cb7` with `…cb8` notify, `…cba` write-without-response, `…cb9` read/notify/write-without-response. That last one matches the Dialog **Serial Port Service** (DSPS) as recalled from Dialog's documentation: `cb8` is device-to-phone data, `cba` is phone-to-device data, `cb9` is flow control. Confirm against Renesas' docs before relying on it. If right, the readings are a serial byte stream on `cb8`, not a tidy "level" characteristic, and the device may wait for the phone to write something before it sends.
- **Every read timed out**, even Manufacturer Name, and the link dropped exactly 30.0 s after the first unanswered request, on both attempts. Nothing was ever received.
- Pairing is not the reason: the owner saw no pairing prompt, the official app is installed on the same iPhone, and the device is not listed under iPhone Settings → Bluetooth. `LeakVue_0245` is also the name the official app shows (each unit has its own number).
- Working theory, unconfirmed: the firmware simply never answers reads (on Dialog's stack the application has to answer each read itself, and the official app presumably never reads). Bluetooth allows one outstanding request at a time and iOS disconnects 30 s after an unanswered one, so the Lab's automatic read of C1 doomed each connection and blocked everything queued behind it, including the subscribes.
- The Lab was reworked for this: subscribe only, reads on tap, firmware service untouched, and the opt-in DSPS "ready" byte.
- **Second capture, reworked Lab (same day):** the theory held. All three subscribes (`C18`, `C19`, `C21`) were answered within about 60 ms each, and with no reads sent the connection was still up 37 s later when the log was shared. The device immediately notified `02` on the flow-control characteristic `…cb9` (in DSPS, `01` = flow on, `02` = flow off, so this is the device saying it is not accepting data from the phone yet). Nothing arrived on the data characteristic `…cb8`. The owner had not yet tapped "ready" in that capture.
- **Third capture (same connection, continued):** the phone wrote `01` to `…cb9` twice. **No data ever arrived on `…cb8`** in the three minutes that followed. A read of `…cb9` was answered at once with `01` (so the serial-port profile answers reads; only the Device Information reads were seen to hang). The device dropped the link 238.5 s after connecting, which looks like a 4-minute idle timeout. So the LeakVue 2 does not stream on its own: it waits for a command, presumably on `…cba` (or through the unidentified `866d3b04…` service, whose three characteristics have not been read).
- **Stop-and-ask resolved (26 Nov 2026):** the owner confirmed ALD/PlainSight won't help and explicitly authorised reverse-engineering the protocol. It's the owner's own hardware, the DSPS link is plaintext (no encryption/bonding to defeat), so this is interoperability by observing their own device's traffic — not an attack on the official app. The agreed, non-invasive method is to **watch the official app's own conversation** (no decompiling, no guessed writes). - **Reading frame decoded (`LV.decode.leakvue`, tests in tests/decode.test.js).** Each `…cb8` notification is **5 bytes**: `b0 b1 b2 b3 b4`. `b4` is a packet counter (+1/frame, perfect across 318 steps; `leakvueGap` measures loss). `b0..b3` are the laser level as a **big-endian** value (`raw` = uint32 BE); `b0` sits at `0x84` in normal still use and rises to `0x88` during big excursions. `ff 7f ff ff` = sensor error / no target. Confirmed against the owner's own app screenshot: in still water the value was dead flat for 28 s, and during the sensor-waving spikes the big-endian value rose — matching the official graph going **up**. (b1/b2 alone, with b0 as a flag, moves the opposite way, so b0 is part of the magnitude, not a flag.)
